# Changelog

layerling started over at 1.0.0 when it was forked from SketchForge-3D 1.0.9.
Everything from 1.0.9 downwards is SketchForge's history, kept here because the
code still carries it - so a lower number further down is older, not newer.

## Unreleased

- **Label shelves for Gridfinity bins (#455):** A Gridfinity bin had plain inner walls with no place for a label. A bin of 3 height units or more can now have a label shelf along any of its four walls, several at once: **Label shelf at the back**, **Label shelf at the front**, **Label shelf on the left** and **Label shelf on the right**. A shelf is flat on top, with a 1.2 mm strip down at the front and an underside rising at 36 degrees, and sits 0.5 mm under the feet of a bin stacked on the lip. Each shelf has its own **Shelf width** (14 mm), **Shelf length** (0 runs it from wall to wall) and, when it is shorter, **Shelf position** along its wall. Two shelves that meet in a corner join into one, and where a shelf meets a wall or another shelf, **Shelf corners** rounds or chamfers the corner by **Corner size** (2 mm; 0 leaves it sharp). The bin stays an exact body for the STEP export. Also through the MCP action `layerling_create_shape` with `gridfinityLabelBack` and the other `gridfinityLabel` settings. Suggested by @9ballrider. Contributed by @mlugo-apx.

## 1.87.0

- **Turkish interface (#511):** The interface is available in Türkçe, translated by @murartdursun25 with the help of Claude and read by him as a native speaker: 2547 of 2562 sentences; the others and the chapters of the guide show in English until they are translated.
- **The shape library no longer meets an invisible edge when it is moved (#509):** The library follows the toolbar size with a zoom of its own (0.75 for small, 1.25 for large), and the code that moves a panel took no notice of it. With the small toolbar the library stopped a good way before the right edge of the window and was cut short at the bottom long before the window's edge; with the large toolbar it could be pushed out of the window on the right and below. The position now counts in the panel's own zoom, so the library goes as far as the window's edge on every toolbar size, and when it is pulled down it only gets shorter, down to its title bar, so that it stays inside the window. The same count keeps a library that is pulled wider from its left edge standing on its right edge (#505).
- **The view can be panned to parts far beside the plate (#502):** The point the view pans and turns around was held to the plate, and only went further after "Center view on selection" had taken it to a part. A design with parts parked far to one side of the plate could not be panned over to them with the mouse. It now reaches every body of the design, out to the far side of the farthest one, as it already did upwards (#425). The plate stays the least range. Shown in a video by @gquiring, whose parts lay far to the left of the plate.
- **A letter edge for the Text shape (#499):** The name tag could round or chamfer the top edge of its letters, up to 5 mm, but a plain text had only the **Bevel**, which rounds about 1.8 mm at its largest. The Text properties now hold the same setting, **Letter edge**: None, Round or Chamfer, with a size (0.4 mm to start with, at most as high as the text and 5 mm). It builds the same smooth skin as on a name tag, works for every font, fill and curved text, and is what the export writes. A rounding goes as far as the strokes are wide: where a stroke is thinner than twice the size, the edge ends in a narrower top. The underside stays flat for letters that are glued on afterwards. While an edge is chosen it takes the place of Bevel and Segments. The `letterEdge` of the MCP set it already. Asked for by @KenoGuy.
- **Keep colours for good (#507):** The colours you mix yourself were saved on their own under "Recently used", but only eight, and the oldest dropped out when a new one came. A pin button next to the new row **Kept** keeps the colour you have chosen: it stands in a row of its own, newer colours never push it out, and the same button lets go of it again. Up to 21 colours are kept, in rows of seven, in this browser like the recent ones; palette colours do not need it, and a colour that is kept is not shown twice. It works for one body and for several. Suggested by @prmod3d.
- **The ruler's handles pull it as far as its slider (#500):** The handles of a body stop at the size of the plate (at least 220 mm), but the Length slider of the ruler goes up to 500 mm, so a ruler could be dragged no further than 220 mm and typed or slid to more. A ruler only measures and is never printed, so its handles now go as far as the slider: 500 mm, or the plate if that is larger.
- **The shape library can be pulled to any size (#505):** The library that the Shapes button opens had one width (three to five columns, by toolbar size) and a height of at most 650 px. Its edges and corners now pull the size, as wide and high as the screen allows, which suits very large and very small monitors; a left edge moves the panel so that its right edge stays. The size is kept in the browser, a double-click on an edge sets it back, and the edges also answer the arrow keys. Pulled narrower than 300 px the library leaves out the names and shows only the pictures, as Tinkercad does; every tile carries its name as a tooltip. The sketch's shape menu keeps its size. The pin in the title bar is larger now (36 px instead of 28, the symbol 20 instead of 16), as @prmod3d found it hard to hit on a high-resolution screen. Asked for by @ARSoderberg and seconded by @prmod3d.
- **Reset grid (#503):** Someone who had set the grid colour to that of the plate, and the darker lines too, saw no grid at all, and a new design looked the same: the settings saved with "Make default" are kept for the whole browser, and removing the browser does not clear them. The Workplane page of the settings now ends with **Reset grid**. It puts the grid's visibility, colours, spacing and line weight back to what the app starts with, for the design you are in and in the default saved with "Make default", so new designs start with a visible grid again. The size of the plate and the printer are not touched; an inch grid goes back to 1/4 in. Asked in a question from @Fixer322.
- **A tapered or leaning cylinder keeps its turn when it is grouped (#504):** A round cylinder that was tapered or leaning and then turned by 90 degrees came out of Group, Export and the Boolean tools as if it had never been turned, so the group had other overall measures than the parts had on the plate. The turn of a round body was dropped because a plain cylinder looks the same turned about its own axis, but a taper, a lean or a twist follows the body's own axes. The turn is kept for such a body now, in the mesh and in the STEP yaw.
- **`sitemap.xml` is no longer kept in Git (#498):** The file is written by the build on every `npm run dev` and `npm run build`, but it was also committed, so a tree that had been built could refuse `git pull` with "your local changes to apps/web/public/sitemap.xml would be overwritten". It is in `.gitignore` now and made by the build, like the other generated guide files; the deployment and the static export build it themselves. If you still get the error once after updating, run `git restore apps/web/public/sitemap.xml` and pull again. Reported by @foofoodog; @freddysae0 explained the cause.
- **Names of controls under a hovering Apple Pencil (#495):** An Apple Pencil can hover above an iPad screen, and Safari reports it as a pen that moves without pressing, but it shows no tooltip for it, so the names of the buttons stayed hidden. A pen that rests on a control for a moment now shows its name in a small bubble, the text a mouse tooltip shows; pressing, moving away, scrolling or a key hides it again. It is only active on an iPad or iPhone, where the browser shows nothing itself; on other devices the browser's own tooltip works for a pen as before. Asked by @DarkKnightForensics.
- **Print the list of keyboard shortcuts (#490):** The window with the shortcuts, in the editor and in sketch mode, has a printer button next to the question mark. It opens the print dialog of the browser with only the list on the page, black on white and in large type, which also makes it a clear PDF through "Save as PDF"; Ctrl+P while the window is open does the same. It prints the list for the mode you are in. Asked by @WimSnor, who had been taking screenshots of the list and found the colours made them blurry.
- **The whole user guide in Polish (#501):** All 17 chapters are translated, with every button named as the Polish interface names it. The Polish tape measure is called "Zmierz", as in Tinkercad, and the sentences for bringing shapes back from the history are in. The language stays marked as not complete until a second Polish reader has looked at it. Besides that, the letter "ł" is now treated as "l" in the addresses of guide headings, in the command search (Ctrl+K) and in the search of the guide, so "polóz" finds "Połóż" and a heading like "Własne czcionki" gets the address `wlasne-czcionki`. And two places in the guide that showed a raw `{count}` in every language (the darker grid line "every 10 grid steps" in chapter 3 and "Show earlier versions" in chapter 1) read right now. Contributed by @cyryllo, who also found the three problems.
- **`layerling_save_project`: the MCP saves the editable design as a .lyl (#494):** An AI could write STL, 3MF and OBJ to a path through the MCP, but the editable project only through the browser's save dialog. The new tool takes a `path` (a missing .lyl ending is added), `overwrite` (an existing file is only replaced when it is true) and `history` (steps of undo history, 0 for none, or `all`), packs the project as the export window does and writes it, through a temporary file, at that path on the computer where the MCP server runs. The answer gives the full path, the size and how many objects and history steps the file holds. The target of Ctrl+S in the editor is not touched. Suggested by @FrankMozingo on GitHub.
- **The Crescent no longer comes out hollow where it overlaps another body (#493):** The two lids of the Crescent were turned the wrong way against its walls, so the mesh had edges that did not meet. A slicer then treated the part where the Crescent overlaps another solid as a hole, and the merge in the export (several bodies into one) failed for it, so the Crescent was written as a separate overlapping shell. Lids and walls now face outwards together, the mesh is closed, and a Crescent merges with other bodies like any other shape. A test now checks that every edge is walked once in each direction. Reported by @uowmag, who saw the overlap of a Crescent and two cylinders hollow in OrcaSlicer.
- **Hidden shapes show a number, and a design says so when it is opened (#489):** The count of hidden shapes stood only inside the Visibility menu ("Show all hidden (n)"), so someone who hid a shape, left the design and came back had nothing to tell them. A small number now sits on the button of the Visibility group while something is hidden, and a click on it shows everything again. A design that is opened with hidden shapes says so once, with a button "Show all"; a message may carry one button now. The shapes are not unhidden on opening: hiding is saved with the design on purpose. A design opened again in the same session without reloading gets the number only. Reported by @rondlh, who knows Tinkercad's light for hidden objects.
- **The hide button stays when several bodies are selected (#491):** The eye in the title bar of the properties window was only there for a single body. For two or more it is back beside the lock: it hides all the selected bodies, and shows them again when all of them are hidden, as Ctrl+H does. Reported by @rondlh on GitHub.
- **The height value also stands at the height handle (#488):** While the top middle handle was hovered or pulled, the height stood only at the vertical edge that is furthest right on the screen (a wish from the forum, so that the number stays off the body), often far from the handle, and it left the view when you zoomed in on the middle of the body, with no way to type a value. A second number now stands right at the handle, above it (below, for the lower handle), and can be clicked and typed like the one at the edge. The measure at the edge is unchanged, and with Dimensions always visible only that one shows. Reported by @rondlh on GitHub.
- **Bottom edges in the Fillet and Chamfer tools (#473):** The tools had a button **Top edges** that picks only the edges along the very top of a body, but nothing for the bottom, so a fillet or chamfer on the foot alone meant clicking the lower edges one by one. **Bottom edges** is its mirror: it picks only the edges along the very bottom. The MCP action for edge treatments takes `"bottom"` for `edgeIds` next to `"all"`, `"top"` and `"vertical"`, and the guide and the MCP skill say so. Asked by @gquiring, who had to cut a part in two to round only one end; the Rounded Box's own **Top/bottom fillet** stays one value for both.
- **A shape on the pointer shows no measures at the old place (#463):** A custom shape - and parts picked up with C - hanging on the pointer showed their measures and handles at the middle of the plate, where the parts still stand in the design, until the click set them down. They stay out while the parts are carried now. Reported by @t9vh6j9rt4-collab on GitHub.
- **Arrow keys: the view follows only a body that would leave the picture (#486):** The arrow keys move a body along what the screen shows, and the view takes the same step when a body would be pushed past the edge, so that it stays in sight. That rule also fired for a body that already lay at or past the edge, for example after zooming in far: the body stood still on the screen and everything else, the plate and the other bodies, slid past, which felt reversed. Now the view follows only a body that is in the picture and would leave it; one that already lies outside simply moves. Reported by @rondlh.
- **The tablet chapter says when a tap on empty space does not clear the selection (#487):** The table said only "Tap empty space". With Touch rotate on, one finger turns the view instead, and in Select multiple the first tap on empty space only ends the mode and leaves the selection as it is. Both are now in the row.
- **A typed measure is kept when you click beside the box (#486):** In the box beside a body, and in the angle box, a value you had typed was thrown away by a left click on the empty plate and applied by a right click, the wrong way round. The click on the plate takes the selection away, and with it the box, before the box could lose its focus and apply the value; a right click selects nothing, so there it did. Now any press outside the box applies the value first, with the left button as with the right. Esc still cancels. Reported by @rondlh.
- **Alignment handles keep apart and stay when you zoom in (#486):** The nine handles of Align stand where the parts line up, so on small parts, or seen from afar, the three of an axis ran into one blob that could not be picked, and zooming in far enough took all nine away at once, because one corner of the selection lay too close to the camera. The three handles of an axis now keep at least 28 pixels from each other on the screen, and each handle is judged by its own place, so those still in front of the camera stay. Reported by @rondlh.
- **"Save as default" keeps the colour, too (#483):** Suggested by @bdeboer58, who would like to see colours for the shapes of the library. The pictures in the library stay as they are, but the colour is now part of what the button **Save as default** in the Properties panel keeps for a kind of shape: colour a cylinder green, save, and every new cylinder starts green; **Reset defaults** gives the app's own colour back, and the other kinds keep theirs. **A flaw found on the way:** inside one design, a second **Save as default** and **Reset defaults** did not reach the next new shape, because the editor took the change for an echo of its own settings and kept the old ones until the page was reloaded. The default of a size was affected as well as the colour. The settings window used the same route and passes it the same way now.
- **A copy can be moved with the mouse and copied again (#484):** Duplicate, move the copy, duplicate again repeats the step (1.10.0). It only held when the copy was moved with the keys: with the mouse, the press landed on the copy and on the original it lies on at the same distance, the ray took whichever came first in the list - the original - and selected and moved that one, so the chain of copies was gone and the next copy stayed at the old place. Among bodies that lie at the same spot, a press now means the one already selected. Reported by @willi1955 on GitHub.
## 1.86.2

Reported by @prmod3d for "Center view on selection"; the same held for every greyed-out button (Copy, Delete, Group, Fillet and the rest, 30 in an empty design). - **A number at the mouse pointer counts the selection (#481):** Suggested by @prmod3d. ## 1.86.1

- **A number on the edge of a body lying on its side changes the right measure (#480):** With a workplane on a face, 1.81.0 matched the numbers on the horizontal edges to the body's own sides for a body turned by a quarter, but not the number on the vertical edge. A Round Roof turned by 90° about X and given a new height by that number changed its depth or width instead, because the two swap places when the body lies down. Every mark - width, depth and height - is now matched to the side it stands at. Reported by @SteveGork on GitHub.
- **The tablet hint now also shows on an iPad, and in the editor (#467):** The card also floats up in the editor now, after a quarter of a minute, once (closing it on either page closes it for good): whoever opens layerling on a tablet often lands straight in a design, for the installed app opens the last one, and never saw the card on the start page. **Detection:** The card that points to the guide chapter for tablets looked at the media queries for a touch screen without a mouse. Safari on an iPad has answered those differently from one iPadOS version to the next, with a trackpad or a Pencil attached, so on an iPad the card stayed away. An iPad and an iPhone are now recognised by what they call themselves (a Mac with several touch points, or an iPhone/iPad), as the file picker already did; an Android tablet is still recognised by the queries, and a computer with a touch screen and a mouse still sees no card.

## 1.86.0

- **A clear recommendation to install layerling as an app:** The small strip on the start page is now a card that is meant to be seen: as an app layerling starts faster, works without internet, has its own icon and window and does not have to be loaded again each time, and nothing is lost, as layerling still tells you when a new version is out. Where the browser has an install button it is on the card; "Not now" hides the card for two weeks, the cross for good. In the editor the same card appears at the foot of the work area after half a minute of work, so it never gets in the way of the first clicks, and it steps above the support reminder when both are there. Its icon is the same size as the one on the backup notice. It still shows only where installing works (https), never inside the installed app, and not in an ordinary browser tab either once the app has been installed or opened on this computer. The guide's chapter on installing says why it is worth it.
- **The view follows bodies set high above the plate (#425):** With the height no longer held to 220 mm, a body typed in high above the plate could not be centred: Zoom to the selection (F) let it slip off the top edge, because the camera's pivot was held to 70 mm below and 120 mm above the plate. The pivot now reaches to the top of the highest body and the foot of the lowest. Found from a report that bodies at that height could hardly be picked or resized; picking itself worked in my tests up to 900 mm, so that report is not settled.
- **An open sketch line points to Stroke (#478):** Extrude Sketch needs a closed outline and said only to close a loop, although an open line can be built as a stripe with a width: tick "Build as a stroke" in the Finish section. The message now says so, in every language. Asked by @Cowski4444, who wanted Tinkercad's three-sided box. **French:** the stroke was called "Accident vasculaire cérébral" and "Construire comme un coup" (a stroke in the medical sense); it is "Trait" and "Construire comme un trait" now, and "Poste" and "Se termine" in its panel are "Position" and "Extrémités".
- **A twisted star stays straight (#476):** A star twisted about its height turned about the middle of its box, not about its own middle. A five-pointed star is not centred in its box (it reaches further up than down), so its middle circled round the axis and the tips came out at different distances: with a twist of 360 degrees they reached 30 mm from the axis at the bottom and 35 mm in the middle. The star now turns about its own middle in the editor, in the exact body and in the exports; taper works about the same point. Other shapes keep turning about the middle of their box. Reported by @RobbieKnobbie on GitHub.
- **The "What is new" card says it is an excerpt, and links the release notes:** The cards show a selection of what came with a version. A note at the top of the card, and of the list shown before an update, now says that a release can contain more, smaller changes and bug fixes, and links the release notes on GitHub, where everything is listed.
- **F zooms to the selection (#465):** Suggested by @WagnerBoyzRacing, who came from Tinkercad and found F taking him to the middle of the grid, with the selection one Shift away. F now looks at what is selected and, with nothing selected, still brings the whole scene into view; Shift+F works as before. The whole scene is always shown by [Home] and the house button, whose tooltip now names that key. The sketch behaves the same. This changes what F does while something is selected.
- **Interactive Help finds the right place for the buttons that missed it (#424):** Reported by @prmod3d, who went through the program button by button. Some places found nothing (visibility options, the command search, the design name, the footer links, the snap grid list, the mm and in switch) and some led to the wrong section (Paste, Delete, Ungroup, Workspace settings, Theme, Rename design, Hide selected and more), because the guide did not write these names or another section wrote them more often. Chapter 1 has a new section, **The buttons around the work area**, in every language, with one short entry for each and one for the footer; the snap grid list, the mm | in switch and the version link now ask the guide for the right name. More places are to follow as they are found.
- **A line round a traced sketch no longer times out (#461):** A sketch from an SVG with hundreds of small curves stopped with "OpenCascade timed out while building the sketch" once a line was set round it. The kernel could not move the outline, so the line was worked out as straight pieces, 32 for every curve - 29 000 edges for a bulldog drawing - and the kernel's validity check alone took 51 s of a 56 s build. Curves are now cut only as finely as 0.01 mm needs (a short curve gets 4 pieces, a long one at most 32), and a sketch of more than 1 500 edges is checked by its volume, not by the validity check. The same drawing builds in 1-5 s.
- **A design too big to save says why, and is saved anyway (#475):** A design with very large meshes and a long history failed to save after almost every change, with the bare text "Could not save design shapes", and every change felt slow. Now the message names the reason when it is one you can act on: the design is too big for the project file (delete the history or simplify the heaviest bodies), or the browser has no room left. And when only the whole history is too much, the stored copy in the browser keeps the newest steps that fit, so the design is saved at all; the message says how many steps it kept, once per design. Undo in the open session still has every step. Reported by @grmonsey on GitHub.
- **French: five more shape names (#406):** Suggested by @medmed58, who works with these words: the screw-head cutters are holes, so "Trou lamé", "Trou fraisé" and "Trou doublement fraisé" instead of "Lamage", "Fraisage" and "Double fraisage"; the bent tube is "Tube cintré" (the craft word for a bent pipe, not "courbé"); the round roof is "Demi-cylindre". The guide and the start page follow, and the old words still find the shapes in the command search.
- **A custom shape hangs on the pointer (#463):** A click on a tile of the custom shapes put the shape into the design at once, at the middle of the workplane; only dragging it out of the list chose the place. Now it hangs on the pointer like the shapes of the library: it follows it, also over the faces of other bodies, a click sets it down and Esc puts it away. With "click to place" switched off in the settings it is still put at the middle at once. Reported by @t9vh6j9rt4-collab on GitHub.
- **A chapter of the guide for tablets, and a hint on the start page (#467):** What a tablet needs was spread over the guide: the long press menu, Select multiple, turning with one finger, the Touch rotate switch, the stylus. The new chapter **Tablet and touch** (chapter 17) puts it in one place, with what to know on an iPad: install it as an app from the Home Screen, that Safari deletes what a website stored after seven days without a visit unless it is a Home Screen app (as WebKit describes it), downloads instead of saving into a folder, and **Back up all**. It is there in English, German, Dutch, French, Russian, Czech and Slovenian; the others show the English text. On a device whose main input is touch, a strip on the start page points to it once: it has a button to the chapter, a cross, and is gone for good after either. A computer with a mouse never shows it. Suggested after a question from an iPad user.
- **The Face tool starts from its defaults (#471):** It opened with the values of its last use, even when nothing was created, so an inset that fitted one face came along to the next, where it could be too wide and leave little of the face, or nothing. Each time it opens it now starts from an inset of 0, no corner radius, a depth of 2 mm and **Out: add material**; choosing another face while it is open keeps the values. Contributed by @mlugo-apx.
- **Delete face (#451):** A fillet, a hole or a boss that came with a part imported as STEP could not be taken off in layerling at all, and one in a group an edge treatment had turned into one body only by taking back that treatment and every one after it. A new tool in the Modify group, **Delete face**, takes them off: click the faces of what should go (a picked face lights up, another click takes it out again), then **Delete** or the Delete key, and the faces around them grow until the gap is closed. The body stays exact, so Fillet, Chamfer and the STEP export work on it as before; the deletion is one undo step and is listed with the fillets and chamfers under **Undo an edge feature**, where it can be taken back. Where the faces around them cannot close the gap, such as a main side of a box, nothing changes and the panel says why. Deleting only the floor of a pocket makes it a hole straight through. On a detailed imported part a few faces can take the CAD kernel too long; after 45 seconds the deletion is stopped and nothing changes. It needs exact faces: whatever layerling keeps as a mesh (an imported STL, OBJ or 3MF, a split, wrapped or simplified body, a very detailed shape such as a long text) gets a message saying so. The ribbon has one more button, so its three width steps moved to 2081, 1765 and 1615 px. Also through the MCP actions `layerling_list_faces` and `layerling_delete_faces`. Contributed by @mlugo-apx.

## 1.85.0

- **A width for an SVG or a picture in the import window (#448):** An SVG came in at its own size and had to be resized afterwards, where Tinkercad asks for the size before the drawing lands on the plate. The import window has a field **SVG and picture: width in mm** now: type the width, choose the file, and the drawing is scaled to it, its sketch with it, so the body is built clean at that size. Empty keeps the own size (a picture 50 mm wide as before). The width counts for the next import only and is empty again after it. Through the AI bridge `layerling_import_file` takes `svgWidth`. Asked for by @shanni1783 on GitHub.

It is one step that Undo takes back; the history before stays as it was. For an AI there is `layerling_restore_from_history`, which also takes a part from inside a group. By @gogades.
- **French: dovetail, knurl, teardrop, name tag and icon tag renamed (#406):** Read by @jeromefc50, who finds "Queue d'aronde" a word of carpenters: the dovetail is "Prisme trapézoïdal", the knurl "Cylindre moleté", the teardrop hole "Goutte d'eau" (it is not always a hole), the name tag "Badge nominatif" and the icon tag "Badge d'icône". The old words still find the shapes in the command search. The guide has the new headings; its text still says "balise" and "étiquette" in places.
- **Select multiple for a tablet (#337):** A tablet has no Shift key, so only one body or all of them could be selected. ## 1.69.0

- **Hollowing a block with a pocket:** A block with a hole that does not go all the way through - a box grouped with a raised hole - could not be hollowed with its top open (the default): the kernel's thick solid does not take a face with a pocket in it, and the message said the body could not be hollowed with any wall. Where it fails, the body is now grown over the open face, hollowed from its inward offset, and the cavity cut out of the body as it was - the way a closed body is hollowed. The result is exact: for a 20 x 10 x 20 block with a 10 x 10 pocket 5 deep and a 1.5 mm wall it has the volume worked out by hand. The same goes for the bottom, both ends and a hole right through. Reported in the forum as a wish for mould making (#302).
- **Ten more printers, 200 in all:** Creality Ender-3 V4, Ender-5S, CR-10 V3, CR-M4, CR-M4 SE and K2 SE, Elegoo Neptune 2D, Anycubic 4Max Pro 2, Sovol SV01 Pro and Flashforge Guider 3 Ultra, with the plate and height from OrcaSlicer. The list is looked at against OrcaSlicer's once a week now, so new printers and corrected plates do not go unnoticed.
- **The printers are in alphabetical order:** In the workspace settings, under Workplane, the list of printers followed the order of the slicer's profiles: Bambu Lab had its A1 mini before its A1, with the P1P and X1 in between and the H2 models last. The makers and the models of each maker are sorted now, numbers counted as numbers (CR-6 SE before CR-10, Kobra 2 before Kobra 3). The list of profiles itself is unchanged (#305).
- **Text from LightBurn comes in whole, nearly:** An SVG with text made in LightBurn lost half of its letters on import. Their outlines carry points along the straight edges, the extrusion put a triangle of no area at each of them, and a letter with such a triangle was left out as no solid. Points that lie on the line between their neighbours are now dropped when the outline is read, before the extrusion. In the file of the report 18 of 20 letters come in now, where 10 were left out. The remaining two have an outline that runs along itself and still need joining in the drawing program, as the message after the import says (#304).
- **A thread profile of your own: flank angle, depth and core diameter:** A new **Custom (trapezoid)** under the thread's profile: a tooth with a flat crest and a flat root of the same width, set by the **flank angle** (the angle between the two flanks, 10 to 90 degrees, 60 is the V thread) and the **thread depth**, or the **core diameter**, which is the diameter minus twice the depth - changing one changes the other. At 90 degrees or less no flank is flatter than 45 degrees, so the thread prints without supports; machine screw profiles are standardised but not made for 3D printing. The depth is limited by the angle, the pitch and the diameter, follows a change of the pitch and of the scale, and the play and chamfers work as with the other profiles. Through the MCP bridge: `threadProfile` `custom` with `threadFlankAngle` and `threadDepth`. The own profile is built in the mesh and in the exact body, in a nut, a tapped hole, a rod and a screw. Suggested in the forum.
- **A thread chamfer of any width above zero counts:** A chamfer of 0.001 mm made it vanish, because only more than 0.001 mm counted. A tiny one is a cone that opens the mouth of a tapped hole or nut to exactly the outer diameter and cuts the crests at the rim - good for a hole whose thread starts a few millimetres behind the opening, with a plain bore of that diameter in front. Now any width above 0 builds it, in the mesh and in the exact body. 0 still means no chamfer. Suggested in the forum.
- **Zooming near the top edge no longer jumps out:** With the pointer close to the top of the view, in a view seen from the side, the mouse wheel zoomed far out instead of in. The wheel zooms towards a point under the pointer, and a ray that runs almost level met the plate thousands of millimetres away; the camera was then pulled back onto the plate and the picture with it. Such a point is now left alone, and the zoom goes on around the old pivot. Reported in the forum.
- **Sliders snap to the grid:** Dragged with the mouse, the slider of a length - the height of a box, say - now snaps to the snap step, so it comes out in whole millimetres instead of steps of 0.81. Not with the grid off, not on short sliders, and a typed value and the arrow keys on the slider stay exact. Suggested in the forum.

## 1.67.0

- **SVG import says what it left out (#258):** An SVG shape that does not build into a solid, such as an outline crossing itself or touching itself at a point, was dropped without a word, so a drawing came in with a gap nobody could explain. The import message now says how many shapes of the file were left out and what to try. The guide has it under adding geometry. Follows from the closing sliver found by stainlessdmc12.
- **SVG import: a letter closing a hair beside its start (#258):** A traced logo (letters F, T and L from Affinity) came in without its T. The program writes every number with three decimals, so the T's outline ended 0.001 beside where it began, and the closing edge between the two was a sliver the extrusion could not triangulate. - **Guide: hollow bodies that end in a point (#263):** Hollow needs a flat face to leave open, and a closed cavity fails at a sharp tip too, as on a cone or a revolved triangle. - **Herringbone gear (#265):** A new gear type, the herringbone gear: two helical halves turning opposite ways, like an arrow. It centres itself on its shaft and has no axial thrust, which suits 3D printing, where a helical gear pushes sideways. It takes the helix angle and the quality of the helical gear, the angle applying to each half, and the teeth turn exactly at the middle ring. It stays a mesh for the edge tool and the STEP export. The gear list, the workspace defaults and the MCP bridge (`gearType: "herringbone"`) know it, and the command search finds it as "herringbone" and "Pfeilrad". Asked for on GitHub.
- **Threads: play along the axis (#256):** The clearance of a nut or tapped hole works on the diameter and opens the flanks of a V thread to only about 0.58 of its value, so a vertical gap between the flanks took a wide diameter and brought a sideways slop; printing a pair worked at 0.5 mm but rattled. A new value, **Play along the axis**, makes every tooth of the part thinner along the axis and leaves the diameter alone, so the two can be set apart: 0.2 mm in the diameter and 0.2 mm along the axis give a gap of 0.32 mm between the flanks with only 0.1 mm of air to each side. It is 0 for every saved thread, goes up to a fifth of the pitch, exists for nut and tapped hole, and reaches the display, the edge tool and the STEP export (a Whitworth thread with play goes there as straight pieces instead of the true arcs). The guide has a new section on threads for 3D printing, with the consequences and tips. Workspace defaults and the MCP bridge (`threadAxialClearance`) take it too. Asked for by hlmodtech on GitHub.
- **Sketch: shapes pushed over each other (#239):** Two closed shapes pushed over each other showed a hole where they overlap, while the body became their union, and dropping them made a shared point where their lines cross, which welded the shapes together so they could not be pulled apart again and left stray edges behind. Now the sketch shows what the body will be: a hole only where an outline lies wholly inside another. And moving shapes or points makes no shared points where lines cross; drawing a line across others still does. Reported by DrJones in the forum.
- **Hole pattern: the wall between the holes (#239):** The hole pattern showed only the spacing centre to centre, but what a print needs to know is the wall left between two holes. A new slider, **Wall between the holes**, shows it and sets it: it is the least distance between a hole and its neighbours, for every hole shape and both arrangements, and the spacing follows it and the other way round. Asked for by luk-saw on GitHub.
- **Rounded box: quality 1 is a true chamfer with rounded top and bottom edges too (#239):** The rounding of a rounded box with its top and bottom edges rounded was built from two faces meeting at each edge, so it always had twice the pieces of the quality: quality 1 gave two, no chamfer. Quality 1 now gives one flat 45 degree piece on all twelve edges and a triangle on each corner, as it does for the corners alone. The edge tool takes such a box as a mesh, as the exact round would not match what the view shows. Higher qualities are unchanged. Found by luk-saw on GitHub.
- **T toggles transparency (#276):** The T key makes the selected bodies see-through, and opaque again on the next press. If any selected body is already see-through, all become opaque, otherwise all become see-through, so a mixed selection goes opaque first. Holes and locked bodies stay as they are. - **Sketch editor: Shift+right drag pans, the snap grid control sits over the plate:** Like in the 3D editor, the view of the sketch pans with Shift plus the right mouse button, next to the middle button and Ctrl/Cmd plus right. The snap grid control now floats over the sketch plate instead of having a strip of its own. By gogades (PR #269).
- **Torus: sides and steps (#272):** The torus drew its ring and its tube with fixed numbers of corners (144 and 36), so a big one, 200 mm across, showed facets and there was no way to change it. ## 1.58.1

- **Ring gear and rack:** The gear has two more types. The **Ring gear** has its teeth pointing in - the outer wheel of a planetary set - and is set by teeth, module and backlash like an involute gear, plus the rim round the teeth; its outside is module × (teeth + 2.5) plus twice the rim, with round teeth module × (teeth + 1.7). A pinion of the same module runs in it at a centre distance of module × (ring teeth − pinion teeth) / 2, which the properties panel gives for the selected pair; ring teeth = sun teeth + 2 × planet teeth makes a planetary set. The **Rack** is the gear of endless radius, its involute flanks straight lines at the pressure angle: a bar as long as teeth × π × module, so racks of one module line up end to end, its depth free; a gear of the same module rolls on it with its centre teeth × module / 2 in front of the pitch line, a module behind the tooth tips. Both take round teeth too, both are exact bodies for the edge tool and STEP, and switching the type keeps the module. The MCP bridge takes `gearType: "internal"` with `gearRim`, and `"rack"`. Promised to @prmod3d and @luk-saw in #201.

## 1.8.0

- A thread can carry a different tooth profile now, next to the existing **V** shape: **Trapezoidal** and **Round**, both requested by name in the forum, because a sharp crest and a sharp root print poorly with FDM - the crest rounds itself off in the print and the root becomes a stress riser, exactly what a screw thread should not have. ## 1.6.0

- A screw head can have its rim broken, and it no longer goes through the CAD kernel to get there. Chamfering the head of a screw used to end in a timeout and nothing else: edge treatment has to rebuild the whole body as a CAD solid first, and a thread is a tessellated helix - a plain M6 screw is 13,304 triangles. Measured here: 20 s to sew and heal that into a solid, another 107 s to collect its edges, and what came back were 14,845 edges, one per triangle boundary, with no head rim to be picked out among them. On a slower machine that is minutes of waiting for a list nobody can use. The head carries its own chamfer now, as **Head chamfer** beside the head height - exact, instant, and with no kernel involved. It breaks both rims of the head, the free face and the shoulder the shaft rises from: a screw stands on its head, so a chamfer on the free face alone would sit against the workplane where nobody can see it. It is a cone about the axis, so on a hex head it cuts the corners deeper than the flats, which is what a turned head chamfer looks like. How far it can go depends on the head - the socket, the collar around the shaft and a remaining strip of flank all have to survive - so a hex head, whose corners eat more height, allows less than a cylinder head of the same size. A countersunk head does not offer it at all, because its cone already is one. A screw saved without the value stays sharp, so nothing in an existing design moves by itself.

- The nut has the same chamfer, on both of its faces, as **Rim chamfer**. It is the same cone and the same reasoning: a real nut is chamfered on both sides, and a printed one wants its sharp corners broken just as much. What limits it there is the bore rather than a socket - the thread's own countersink at the mouth eats into the face from the inside, and the chamfer has to stop short of it, leaving a flat ring in between. A nut pulled flatter than the standard height gets the value trimmed again when the shape is built, so the two cones can never meet in the middle.

- Edge treatment says no straight away instead of timing out. The guard it had stood at 180,000 triangles, which is a number from nowhere. Measured on this machine, preparing a mesh costs about 0.6 s at 400 triangles, 6.6 s at 2,208 and 11.1 s at 3,806 - and at 7,612 it had still not finished after four minutes. Collecting the edges grows with the square of the triangle count, because every edge is held against the faces around it, while the watchdog's estimate grew as a straight line; so anything much above a few thousand triangles had been failing all along. It just failed slowly, after a minute of hope and a forced cancel. The tool now stops at 4,000 triangles before any work begins, and the message names the count, the limit, and - for a thread, which is where this comes up - the head chamfer that does the job without the detour. The estimate behind the watchdog follows the measurements now instead of a line.

- A rotated body keeps its properties. Rotating anything but text or a group turned it into a plain mesh: the diameter of a nut, the sides of a cylinder, the teeth of a gear were all gone from the panel, and no way led back. That was not an oversight but a trade - a body is rotated about the centre of its frame, and afterwards the frame has to be measured again from the rotated geometry, or the handles would sit somewhere else than the body. Baking it into a mesh does that measuring. What was missing was the memory: the body's own values had in fact survived on the record all along, only the *kind* had been overwritten, and the panel keys off the kind. It is now written down what the body was and how far it has been turned, so the panel shows its values again. Changing one rebuilds the body, turns it by the remembered angle and measures the frame anew - which also means a rotated body no longer accumulates tessellation, because every change starts from the parameters again. Successive rotations compose as quaternions rather than as added Euler angles, which is where three turns about three axes would otherwise drift apart. Dragging a handle still works on the frame, untouched. A mirrored body is deliberately not remembered: a mirrored right-hand thread is a left-hand thread, and re-applying that honestly is a different job.

- Head height and both chamfers arrive when a screw is created. They were offered by the shape defaults and by the MCP bridge, and both were quietly dropped on the way in: a new screw always came out with the standard head, whatever had been asked for. This turned up while the head chamfer was being wired up, and it was the same omission three times over.

- Ten status messages spoke English in a German window. "Deleted 2 selected shapes" after a delete, and the same for cutting, moving, rotating, snapping to the grid, dropping to the workplane, separating parts and showing hidden shapes; a tenth sat in sketch mode, one line above its translated neighbour. All of them were English sentences written straight into the code instead of keys in the catalogue. They are keys now, with singular and plural, and one further literal that was overwritten a line later by its own German version is simply gone. A test reads the source of the three components that can show a status message and fails on any display string that did not go through the translator - which is the part that makes this stay fixed, because a missing key renders as the key itself and is loud, while an English sentence is invisible to anyone working in English.

Both arrive as ordinary points and edges, the same as the ones you draw - every point can be moved afterwards, every edge split. Their curves are four-point Béziers, which follow the true arc to within a ten-thousandth of its radius.
- And two more after them: a **pie slice** and a **bolt circle**. The slice is a quarter of the circle above it, an arc between two radii. The bolt circle is a disc with six holes on a pitch circle - at the size it arrives in, a 20 disc, a 13 pitch circle and 3 holes, which is clearance for an M3. Its holes are holes because the sketch says so: it counts how deeply each closed outline sits inside the others, and what sits an odd number deep is cut away. That is also why the disc comes along - six circles on their own would extrude into six pillars. Delete it if you want the pattern inside an outline of your own; it is an ordinary loop like any other.
- A body whose edges were rounded or chamfered can be exported as STEP again - with the rounding in it. Treating an edge turns a box into a mesh, and a mesh is not something STEP can hold, so the export skipped it; a design that held nothing else gave up with "No box/cylinder/sphere or imported STEP solids". The exact shape had been lying beside it the whole time: the CAD service stores its result as a B-Rep on the body, and that is what goes into the file now. A rounded 20 cube comes out as six planes, twelve cylinders and eight spheres - real CAD surfaces, not a facetted approximation.
- The export says what STEP can carry, before and after. The panel under **STEP** now names it - boxes, cylinders, spheres and cones, anything with treated edges, bodies that came in as STEP - and says that threads, springs, gears, text and imported meshes go as STL instead. When nothing in a design fits, the message says so in your own language rather than passing through the English line from inside the exporter, and a skipped shape is counted in a sentence that is no longer half English.

## 1.3.0

- Added notes on the workplane. A note stays where you put it, or pins itself to a body and travels along as that body moves, turns and grows. The pin carries a number, a click folds the card open, a switch in **Visibility** hides all of them at once, and nothing of them reaches an exported STL. A note rides inside the design's own history, so undo and redo take it with them, and it travels in the `.lyl` and to the server without anything extra being asked for.
- **Duplicate** in a design's menu makes a copy of it. On the server the file itself is copied - beside the original, under a free name, picture included - so the copy carries the original's geometry down to the byte and nothing is repacked. In your browser the copy becomes a design of its own, with the same shapes, the same history and a preview picture of its own, bound to nothing on the server. The copy of a copy is called "(copy 2)", not "(copy) (copy)".
- The search now covers the whole server folder instead of only the one you happen to be standing in. It used to hide what did not match in front of you and leave the folder tiles untouched, so a design one level down was invisible and unreachable at once. Every hit now says which folder holds it, and that folder is a button: one click and you are there, with the search cleared. Folders are found by name as well, and while you search, the server tile on the start page says how many matches wait over there.
- The workplane can be worked with fingers. One finger does what the left mouse button does: tap to select, drag to move, drag on empty space for a selection box. Two fingers belong to the view - spread them to zoom, move them together to shift the workplane - and putting the second finger down takes back whatever the first one had started, so a pinch never nudges a part. Turning has no gesture of its own; the camera rail carries a switch for it, and that switch only appears on a touch screen. Until now the view could only be turned with the right mouse button and shifted with the middle one, and a tablet has neither.
- Number fields hand over their whole value when you jump into them, ready to be overwritten. A decimal keypad has no arrow keys to place the caret with, and a field five characters wide is not hit digit by digit with a finger: turning "20.00" into "35" meant deleting one character at a time, and a slip left "23.000".
- The MCP bridge can set the taper it had been reporting all along. The values arrived and were then dropped without a word, because everything a command carries is measured against the workspace shape defaults while a taper belongs to the single body - the same shape of bug as the thread and spring settings in 1.2.1. Which shapes ignore a taper is one function now, so the panel and the bridge cannot drift apart. `radius`, the rounding of a box, was dropped from what the bridge reports: the format carries it, but no control and no command sets it.
- Note for older versions: notes travel inside the design's history, which 1.2.2 and older know nothing about. They open such a file and pass the notes over - **and throw them away on the next save**. The format version itself is unchanged.

## 1.2.2

- The status line moved out of the camera rail's column: it floats at the top of the workspace now, beside the view cube, where a whole sentence fits. Down in the corner the two shared a column, and on a flat window - an iPad in landscape with all of Safari's bars - they met.
- It also goes away again. A confirmation steps back after four seconds, a prompt or a failure after thirty, and while there is nothing to report there is no panel at all.
- The camera rail begins sixteen pixels below the view cube instead of sixty-nine, and stands in two columns on a window too flat for one, plus and minus side by side. The rule hangs on the window's height now; the only one before it hung on the width and never matched a window that was wide but flat.

## 1.2.1

- Fixed the workspace settings losing what they had been told about threads and springs. **Shape defaults** offered the fields and the value took effect at once, but the next time layerling started it stood at standard again: the list those settings are checked against had never learned the two shapes that arrived in 1.2.0, and whatever is not on that list is dropped without a word on the way into storage. Every other shape kept its defaults all along.
- The MCP bridge can now build every shape the palette has a tile for - polygons, spheres, cones, pyramids, wedges, roofs, tori, tubes, gears, springs and threads - instead of boxes, cylinders and sketches alone. It takes its list from the same catalogue the shape menu is built from, so a shape added later is offered without anybody remembering to say so.
- What a shape is beyond its size now travels in both directions. A thread can be asked for by diameter and pitch, a spring by turns and wire, a text by its lettering, and the same values can be set again afterwards - a cylinder's side count can finally be pinned once the cylinder already stands there. Changing a thread's diameter moves its width and depth with it, and a head that sat at the standard height for its size moves to the standard for the new one.
- Reading a scene tells what a shape is made of. Until now the answer carried sizes only, so a client read the dimensions of a screw without ever learning whether M4 or M5 stood on it. A round body that has no side count of its own reports the number it is drawn with at its current size, and says that the number follows the size.
- A locked object was a dead end over the bridge: four operations refused to touch it and nothing could release it. The same call may now pass `locked: false`, and `hidden` can be set as well.
- Everything arriving over the bridge is held to the limits the panel uses, so a value cannot enter a design that would then be refused when that design is saved. A pitch of 99 on an M5 arrives as 3.75.

The bridge is the local development server's `/api/layerling-mcp`; static builds and layerling.com do not carry it, and none of this changes what the editor does by hand.

## 1.2.0

- Added threads as a shape of their own, in four forms: a threaded rod, a screw with a socket, countersunk or hex head, a hex nut, and a tapped hole. The thread is cut from the real ISO profile - sixty degrees, with the crest and the root flattened the way the standard prescribes - not from a sawtooth. That is what makes a printed screw actually run in a printed nut.
- M2 to M12 are listed by name, and so are UNC and UNF from #4 up to one inch. Diameter and pitch can also be set freely, and for an inch size the pitch field asks for threads per inch instead of millimetres, because that is the number written on the part. Left-hand threads are a switch, not a separate shape.
- A clearance value sets how much room the thread leaves, so a nut printed at 0.2 mm clearance turns on a rod instead of welding itself to it, and both thread ends can take a chamfer that leads the first turn in.
- In the panel, **Length** means the thread alone and the head has its own height slider, so shortening a screw no longer shrinks its head. The diameter is one slider: dragging a handle in the workspace scales both horizontal axes together, so a thread can never come out oval.
- The tapped hole is a cutting tool like any other. Drag it into a part, group the two, and the part has a thread in it.
- Added springs - a wire wound along a helix, with the number of turns, the wire thickness and the resolution as separate values. The spring fills the box it is given exactly, including the wire at both ends, so a spring of 30 mm measures 30 mm.
- Added the polygon: a prism of three to twenty-four sides, sitting exactly in its footprint. A hexagon inserted at 20 mm measures 20 mm across the flats and is equilateral, not squashed.
- The pyramid now has **Top length** and **Top width** instead of running to a point, which makes a frustum a matter of two numbers. Taper did the same job worse and has been dropped there, along with everywhere else it had nothing to act on.
- Corrected the footprint of round shapes: a cylinder drawn at 20 mm now measures 20 mm, where a low side count used to leave it noticeably smaller - a six-sided one measured 17.32.
- Round shapes now pick their own number of sides, following the diameter, so that no flat sits more than five thousandths of a millimetre off the true circle. A small pin no longer carries the polygon count of a large disc, and a large disc no longer shows its facets. **Sides follow the size** turns the following off and pins the number by hand; existing designs keep the number they were saved with.
- The shape list is laid out in several columns and no longer needs scrolling, and its heading is simply **Shapes**.
- Note for older versions: threads, springs, polygons and pyramid frustums are new shape types in the `.lyl` package. The format version is unchanged, so designs written here still open in 1.1, but a design that contains one of the new shapes does not.
